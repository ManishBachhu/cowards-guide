Myra spoke with Eleanor about my situation, and Eleanor graciously permitted me to stay in a room in the temple dormitory for a cheap price. The current room was reserved for active patient care, so by allowing me to crash in a room within the premises, they could still keep an eye on my condition. Renting me a room was a cheap way for Eleanor to satiate her curiosity about my condition by allowing her to observe me for longer. Hey, as long as we both got something out of it, it was a good deal.

Myra said Eleanor felt some regret since she could not heal me at all. So, as an apology, she cut my treatment bill by a lot and asked me to only pay for the care provided by Myra during my hospital/temple stay. The resultant bill was just below one-third of the previous amount. I didn’t know if this amount was low or high by Earth’s standards, but it was obviously a pay cut for Myra. I told Myra I was feeling a little guilty that she lost some money even after caring for me over a week.

She said it was actually a fair price since they weren’t able to use any skill for my treatment. Apparently, people considered any work that didn’t involve the use of mana as grunt work and paid pennies for it. Now I was feeling sorry for myself. I probably have no mana, right? Is anyone in this world gonna pay me for my work?

Today I moved into the dorm room after a week of treatment. I’m surprised myself that I got better so quickly. The room itself was pretty bare-bones... A window, a straw mattress, a jug of water and a box to keep my stuff. That was all there was to it. Pretty big downgrade even from my bachelor lifestyle on Earth, but hey, even having a roof over my head is a blessing at this point.

Eleanor found a spot for me with the temple’s kitchen staff. They currently had a lot of work because of some upcoming event, so they told me I could help if I pitched in. It’s just a temp job, but it’s a start at least.

Myra showed me the way to the kitchen, where I met the head chef. Even for a temp gig, it was important to register myself under his command.

“Jay, is it?” spoke the head chef, Gustavo, after our introductions. “What kind of cooking skills do you have?”

“I can handle myself with a knife and make some simple dishes like curries or rice items.”

“Ah, good, good. I’m glad you can do that much at least. Some apprentices I got were so bad, they couldn’t even hold the damn knife properly. Ha ha ha.”

Gustavo was smacking my shoulder and pepping me up. Seems like the staff crunch was troubling him, and he didn’t want to let valuable help slip. Maybe this could indeed work out?

“Before I let you join the staff, let’s start with a simple vegetable curry to see what you’re made of, eh? The ingredients required are potatoes, carrots, tomatoes......”

I did not have my phone on me, and there was no paper around to note down his instructions, so I just grabbed a nearby bowl and rushed to the pantry to collect the required vegetables. It was a simple mixed veg curry, so I should be able to manage.

I washed the vegetables and brought them to a table with the chopping board and knives on it. I started peeling the vegetables while glancing at Gustavo. He gave a nod of approval, so at least my peeling seems to be up to par.

After peeling them, I moved on to chopping, starting with the potatoes. I sliced the potato in half first. Then I held the potato in place with my left hand and started slicing it from the right. After reaching the end, I rotated it and made similar cuts perpendicular to the first to get evenly cut cubes. After finishing one potato, I dumped it into an adjacent bowl beside me and moved on to the next one.

Gustavo stopped me after I had finished chopping the second potato.

“Well, I can see that your basic cutting technique is pretty good. The pieces are small and evenly sliced,” he said while rolling a couple of pieces in his palm.

“But why are you cutting them one by one? Ain’t that too slow?”

“Huh, what do you mean? I can try placing both halves near each other and cutting them at once. Though I’m not used to it, so I’d slow down and cut carefully, which means it wouldn’t save a lot of time anyway.”

“Nah, that ain’t what I mean. Why don’t you use ‘Quick Chop’ skill? You should already have it, right, based on how finely you’re chopping.”

“‘Quick Chop’? I don’t have such a skill.”

“Eh? Is that so? You should be able to pick it up easily. Let’s see, hey Johnny, c’mere and chop these veggies,” Gustavo hollered to a pretty scrawny kid at the back. He flinched when he heard his boss call for him and slowly made his way to us.

“Hey Johnny, you learnt Quick Chop last week, right? Mind showing it to Jay so he can pick it up? It’ll also be a good chance for me to observe how much you’ve improved.”

Johnny was apparently a newbie apprentice hired last week. He didn’t even have the basic chops for the job at first, so he was put through hell in his first week. He got yelled at so many times that he now flinches anytime he hears his name called out. Poor kid.

He placed a potato on the board but made no motion to pick up the knife. Instead, he took a deep breath and yelled:

“Quick Chop, activate!”

Instantly a blue screen appeared in front of him. There was an image of a potato and a knife pointing at it. Below that was a button called “Game Start.”

Johnny pressed the button, and immediately the potato started rotating. Beside it, a timer was quickly running down. Johnny started tapping on the knife, which made cuts into the potato on every tap. Once the timer ran out, the potato stopped moving, and a “Game Over” screen flashed, and the screen disappeared.

He then slowly picked up his knife and swung it above the potato a couple of times. Eh? Why? What is he doing?

Miraculously, the potato shook violently as if it were being cut and broke apart into several chunks. The pieces were big and somewhat uneven though.

“Hmm, better than last week, but still a long way to go,” Gustavo remarked. “Chop these into finer pieces.”

“Yes, chef.” Johnny quickly collected his pieces into a bowl and ran back to his place.

“So, what do you think? Can you do it?”

I was speechless. What in the bizarre world was that? He played a mini-game in real life and swung his knife wildly in the air, and that chopped the potato??!

What the heck?!

“Can everyone here do this? Can you do it too?” I asked Gustavo cautiously.

“Yes, of course. Wanna see how good I am at it?” Gustavo didn’t even wait for a reply. He grabbed a few veggies and dumped them crudely on the board. They were all different shapes, but that apparently didn’t matter — he immediately activated the skill.

The same mini-game popped again, but with far more objects this time. Gustavo nonchalantly pressed start game. He was far more fluid in moving the virtual knife around and had quick, rhythmic tapping. He easily cleared the game before the time ran out.

Once the screen disappeared, he picked up the knife and took a big swipe over all the vegetables. Immediately, all of them crumbled into small, evenly sized pieces.

My jaw dropped. What is this cheat skill?

“Ha ha, did you get a good look? A high enough level in this skill saves a lot of time and reduces the mana cost. Now you try.”

He put another potato on the board and moved away. I closed my eyes and took a deep breath. Perhaps my injury caused it to fail before. Maybe now that I’m better, it might activate, right? Maybe it just required an acclimatization period since I’m from Earth?

I opened my eyes, held out my hand, and yelled:

“Quick Chop, activate!”

.........

Nothing popped up.

“Quick chop! Quick chopping! Fast chopping! Ultra fast chopping! Open Quick chop mini-game! Start quick chop! Activate secret skill!”

I yelled out a bunch of random things and made all sorts of gestures with my hands, but nothing worked. I was doomed to be a normie in this world as well.

With a depressed look, I explained my condition to Gustavo and told him how even healing magic didn’t seem to have an effect on me.

“Hmm, Eleanor ma’am did say you have a condition that made you forget your skills, but I didn’t expect it to be this bad,” Gustavo said with a concerned look on his face. “Okay, we might as well go all the way and see how you do on the other steps, eh?”

I nodded and started cutting the remaining vegetables. Gustavo said he’d inspect the final dish and went away to cook other dishes.

After completing the chopping, I went to the stove with a pot, added a bit of oil, and stir-fried the vegetables until they softened. Then I added water and spices as needed, closed the pot lid, and started waiting for the dish to cook.

Meanwhile, another chef walked up to the neighboring stove and placed his pot on it. The steps until stirring and adding salt were the same. However, I saw him activate a new skill — Instant Cook.

This time too a screen popped up, but with a different mini-game. It looked like a game where timing was important. There was a circle with a white bar on it. A section of the circle was orange, and it was rotating. You had to press a button at the exact moment the bar was in the orange zone. Each successful press made the orange arc shorter and faster.

The chef must have been an experienced one. He quickly racked up a lot of points and got a “Game Clear.” While I was appreciating his splendid performance, he opened the lid of his dish. Inside it was a ..... perfectly cooked mixed veg curry?! How though? Man had barely started cooking his dish a few moments ago? I was here much earlier and started cooking before him. How has the curry already finished cooking? I opened my pot to check my dish. The vegetables were still hard and undercooked. Weird, we were using the same stove and vegetables. So how was his done already? What kind of mental, physics-breaking skill was this??

He transferred the contents onto a plate and went away to prepare for the next one, while a servant took the completed dish and left to serve it. That Instant Cook skill allowed the chef to cook the whole thing within a couple of minutes, whereas I could only do it the old-fashioned way. It finally took over 15 minutes, with occasional stirring, to make sure the vegetables cooked evenly and didn’t char.

I presented the completed dish to Gustavo, and he went about testing it like a MasterChef judge. He took a spoon and poked at the vegetables to see if they were soft and cooked well enough. Then he brought a spoonful of the curry near his face to sniff it. I had added some spices like pepper seeds and cardamom out of habit, but his recipe hadn’t mentioned them. Will I be reproached for changing his recipe? He looked satisfied enough, cause he finally tasted it.

“This actually tastes pretty good!” Gustavo exclaimed. The people nearby were shocked. Apparently, it is very rare for a new chef to earn his compliments. Heh, nice. Looks like I’m being treated as a prodigy here, even with my bachelor level cooking talent.

“You took so much time preparing the dish, I thought some of it might have burned black. Surprisingly though, the curry is evenly cooked and tastes even spicier than usual. Good job with it. If only you had some skills to do it quicker though.” Sorry pops, I was born this way. Can’t help what I can’t do.

After agonizing for a bit, he decided that his staff crunch was severe enough that it warranted any amount of extra help he could muster. So I spent rest of the day making more mixed veg curry and other simple dishes.

As I was leaving after completing my work, Gustavo mentioned that he’d call me again tomorrow. I was pretty pleased that I did well enough to impress the boss on the first day itself. Thanks to that, I was able to get a good rest even on a straw mattress.

The next day, I was waiting to hear back from Gustavo, but I didn’t get any call from him, even late into the evening. Weird, I did good, right? How come I haven’t heard anything back from him? This is bad! Every aspiring job-seeker knows this feeling - No news is bad news!
