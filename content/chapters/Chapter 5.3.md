The very next morning, Myra came knocking at my door with a panicked look.

“The higher-ups are calling for you at the High Bishop’s office. It looks bad. You need to come with me at once.”

She dragged me the whole way while I scrambled to gather my thoughts. Eleanor sat at her desk with a calm smile on her face. Beside her stood a number of priests, their expressions ranging from disgust to open amusement at whatever was about to happen. The most striking of them was the priest from yesterday, who now wore an even more outrageous cowl.

“I’ve summoned him as you requested, Brother Ludwin. What is the matter you wished to bring up?” Eleanor asked.

“High Bishop, this man is a thief. He tried to rob the temple in broad daylight. He must be punished immediately.”

“What? I’ve done no such thing. I haven’t touched anything I wasn’t permitted to,” I said at once.

“Silence, you ingrate. Your crimes are obvious to anyone with eyes. How dare you lie in the house of the gods?”

_Has this man gone insane? He won’t even let me get a word in._ I shot a pleading look at Eleanor. She gave a subtle nod and turned toward him.

“I understand your position, Ludwin. I agree he needs to be punished. Tell me what crime was committed, so that I can levy the appropriate penalty.”

_Hey, why has it already been decided that I’m guilty, with only the size of the punishment left to debate?_

“This man stole from yesterday’s collections, despite being warned that his movements were tracked.” Ludwin’s grin was thoroughly cocky. “You surely understand how severe it is to steal what is meant for the goddess. I trust you will not let sentiment cloud your judgment, High Bishop. The temple must make an example of him, so that everyone sees how it treats its malefactors.”

Several of the priests nodded along. Eleanor’s smile stayed exactly where it was.

“Rest assured, Ludwin, I will act accordingly. So tell me, how much was stolen?”

“That... I don’t know for certain. But there is no doubt this scoundrel used underhanded means to blind us.”

“I do not follow. We use merchant’s bells for this very purpose, to track any illicit attempts at theft. Comparing their records with our collections should be a simple matter. How much are we short?”

“Here’s the weird part. We actually have more money than our records indicate.”

“Huh?!” I blurted out. “I’m confused.”

“You claim Jay stole from us,” Eleanor said slowly, “and yet we hold more money than the records indicate?”

_Truly, what a boneheaded accusation of theft. What am I, a magician who steals a coin from someone and to leave them with two in the end?_

“I was stumped at first as well,” Ludwin said, undeterred. “But a look at the logs made it apparent that this man was responsible. The bell records every transaction, along with the name of the employee who collected it. However, not a single transaction by this person appears in the records.”

“That’s impossible. I tapped the bell before and after every payment. I put all the money straight into the box too.”

“Heh. Tapping the bell means nothing. Payments are recorded automatically. All that tapping the bell does is open the menu for customers.”

_What the heck? This man played me from the start._

“But you knew that, didn’t you?” he continued, glaring at me. “You knew how the device works, so you planned to steal from us and erase some transactions so nothing could be traced back to you.”

“That was the first time I’d ever seen that bell. How would I know how things work?”

“More lies. Everyone knows about the bell. Merchants of every class use them, because the government mandates it for auditing and taxes. Everyone has seen one, but few truly know how it works.”

_Damn, are they actually that common?_

“I ... , I lost my memory, so even if I had seen one, I wouldn’t...”

“What a convenient story. I looked into your admission history, and it is riddled with holes. You must be a spy sent to infiltrate us and steal our money and our secrets. Too bad we weren’t as gullible as you thought. We caught onto your scent right away. I will make sure you are severely punished.”

_This is bad. He’s branded me not only a thief but a spy as well. I need to push back before this becomes truly hopeless._

“These are baseless accusations. You claim that I stole from the collections yesterday, but you don’t have any solid proof that I actually did it.” I shot back.

“I have the bell’s records, which show you are the thief beyond a shadow of a doubt,”

“Nonsense, those records only show that my name is missing from them. For all we know, the collection is untouched and this is just an accounting mistake on your part. And how can you claim there are no transactions under my name when you were the one who supervised me yesterday?”

Ludwin turned red and was positively seething. I’d turned the whole thing on him and called his competence into question. Just a bit more.

“How dare you? I knew you were suspicious the moment I saw you. You even hesitated before returning the bell. You tampered with it, didn’t you? It’s a good thing I collected everything back at once. You are a very cunning man.”

“Funny, I was about to say the same of you. Who knows? Maybe you tampered with it yourself, so that you could blame me, get me thrown out, and pocket some change as a bonus. Two birds, one stone. What a cunning plan.”

“That is enough, both of you,” Eleanor intervened, and glared at me. “Mr. Jay, such serious allegations against a member of the cloth will not be tolerated without legitimate proof. Do you have any?”

I shrank back and bowed. “I went too far. I’m sorry.”

“The same goes for you, Brother Ludwin. Keep your accusations to what can be proved.”

“My apologies, High Bishop.” He bowed his head, but his voice never softened. “Still, I must plead with you to remove this man from the premises. Whatever the evidence says, someone like him should not be around us.”

“I heard you the first time. Give me a moment to collect my thoughts.”

Eleanor closed her eyes. I waited in fear for her judgment. She might have a soft spot for me, but she trusted Ludwin far more. If Earth’s Middle Ages were anything to go by, these folks could execute me without proof and no one would bat an eye. _Was I really going to get screwed over for trying to help with good intentions?_

At last, she opened her eyes.

“I shall order a search of Mr. Jay’s room and personal effects. If we find evidence of malfeasance against the temple, we will remove him. If he comes clean, no further action will be taken.”

“High Bishop...” Ludwin began, but she raised a hand.

“Regardless of the result, Jay shall not be allowed near any area involving collections or donations.” Her expression softened as she turned to me. “Until we determine the cause of this incident, we must ensure the safety of the temple. I hope you understand.”
