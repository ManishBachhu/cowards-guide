# Chapter 0

The monitor was the only light in the dim room, illuminating a battle already in progress.

On screen, a warrior with fiery red hair swung a golden sword wreathed in flames. The movements were precise, aggressive—clearly a veteran player. The blade carved through the air toward a flicker of motion at the screen's edge. Fire erupted along its arc, deadly and spectacular.

Alas, it hit nothing.

A poison needle struck the warrior from behind. Then another. The health bar—already riddled with status effects—dropped further into critical range.

The warrior spun, slashing wildly, desperately trying to land at least one clean hit. Any hit. The dragon-slaying blade cut only air while poison ticked away the remaining health.

The warrior collapsed.

Victory screen flashed. A green-haired woman emerged from the shadows, flashing a grin radiating pure satisfaction. The kind of smile that said she knew exactly how annoying her build was.

In the dark room, Jay set down his controller and grinned back at her.

The chat box exploded with his opponent's rage.

"Trash build" "No skill poison spammer" "You're everything wrong with this game"

Jay's grin widened. Perfect.

His phone buzzed. Mark's name lit up the screen.

"Hey Mark, busy with a game. What's up?"

"Just making sure you remember that we have the Finance exam tomorrow morning, dude." Mark's tone carried that familiar mix of exasperation and concern. "You almost missed the last one 'cause you overslept."

Jay glanced at his laptop. A tab was still open with pages of accounting formulas he'd been half-studying between matches. "I should manage. Probably."

"Jay—"

"One more game. That's it. Just downloaded this new one—Path of Pain. Supposed to have insane build diversity."

The sigh on the other end was audible. "Your funeral. Don't blame me when you're asleep in the exam hall."

"Wouldn't dream of it. Thanks for the reminder."

Jay ended the call and minimized his study notes. He'd cram in the morning. The new game icon gleamed on his desktop—a fantasy world promising a thousand ways to play.

He clicked it, high with anticipation.

The screen flashed white, blindingly bright, washing out everything in the room.

When the light faded, there was no one left. Jay was gone.
